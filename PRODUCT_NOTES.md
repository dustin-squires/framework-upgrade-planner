# Product notes

## What I observed

- Framework Laptop 13 is presented as a laptop that can be customized, repaired, and upgraded over time.
- The current product page describes user replaceable memory and M.2 storage, four user selectable Expansion Card slots, and generation over generation processor upgrades.
- The product experience spans multiple laptop generations and component types, so compatibility is a meaningful part of the ownership question.
- Framework’s public product pages explain capabilities, but an owner still has to translate those details into the configuration of the specific machine already in front of them.

## User friction / opportunity

An owner who wants to improve an existing Framework laptop may know the symptom (“I need more room” or “I want a faster machine”) without knowing which component can change, what is compatible, or what they get to keep. A long catalog or generic recommendation list makes that decision harder.

## Why I think this matters

Framework’s ownership model creates a product relationship that is different from replacing a whole laptop. The useful experience should make the machine’s modularity understandable and preserve the owner’s control over the decision.

## Product hypothesis

If we let an owner identify a small amount of information about the Framework Laptop 13 they already have, then show a persistent component view with explicit compatible upgrade paths, they will be able to explore the machine’s future without needing to understand the entire catalog first.

## Evidence vs. assumptions

### Observed

- Framework documents the Laptop 13 as supporting replaceable memory and M.2 storage.
- Framework documents four Expansion Card bays and describes them as user selectable.
- Framework’s current Laptop 13 page lists memory up to 96GB and storage up to 8TB for the current AMD Ryzen AI 300 Series model.

### Assumed / needs validation

- The first useful setup can be limited to model, memory, and storage state.
- Owners will understand a component based view more quickly than a conventional upgrade form.
- A deterministic explorer is more trustworthy for this task than an opaque “best upgrade” score.
- The compatibility rules and available parts need to be verified across every supported Laptop 13 generation before this could represent the full ecosystem.

## Constraints

- This is a prototype, not a claim about Framework’s internal architecture, roadmap, or recommendation systems.
- Only product facts that can be traced to public first party information should appear as facts in the experience.
- Sample records must be clearly labeled when current compatibility or availability is not established.
- The first slice should remain small enough to review in a few hours.

## Questions I would ask the team

- Which owner questions most often precede a memory, storage, or mainboard upgrade?
- How does Framework represent compatibility internally across Laptop 13 generations and regional inventory?
- Where do owners get stuck when identifying the model or current component state?
- What evidence supports describing an upgrade’s benefit beyond the raw characteristic that changes?
- Should Expansion Cards be part of the first ownership journey, or are they better served as a separate customization flow?
